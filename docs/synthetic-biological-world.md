# Synthetic Biological World

The Synthetic Biological World is the controlled computational laboratory of the CEH research program.

It is deliberately constructed so that the generating process is known: latent state, population structure, stable regimes, intervention timing, transition boundary, noise and observation operators.

## Population structure

A world contains a tensor with axes for time, entities and latent features. This permits analysis at entity, population and transition-geometry levels.

## Controlled intervention

The world contains an intervention at a known time. This is a causal challenge inside the computational laboratory, not a claim about a biological mechanism.

## Independent modalities

Different observation operators transform the same latent state into different measurement spaces. A signal that survives independent representations is more interesting than one that exists only in a single representation.

## Falsification

Future benchmarks should include no-transition worlds, transition worlds, intervention/control worlds, noise, missing observations, shuffled time, identity permutations, modality disagreement and held-out future evaluation.

## Ground truth is not evidence

Success here demonstrates only recovery of a known computational construction under specified assumptions. It does not establish that the same phenomenon exists in biological systems.

The progression remains:

Synthetic validity -> Benchmark validity -> Biological data -> Independent replication -> Experimental challenge

A failure at any stage is a scientific result.
