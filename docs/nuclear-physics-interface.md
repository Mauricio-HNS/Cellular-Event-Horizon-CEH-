# Nuclear and Radiation Physics Interface

CEH will explicitly investigate a physical layer connecting radiation physics, biophysics, statistical physics and cellular-state dynamics.

This does not mean that atomic nuclei and cell nuclei are the same physical problem. The relevant bridge is multiscale: physical perturbation, energy deposition, DNA and chromatin response, nuclear state, cellular state and long-timescale transition.

## Physical-to-biological chain

PHYSICAL PERTURBATION
→ ENERGY DEPOSITION / MICRODOSIMETRY
→ DNA + CHROMATIN RESPONSE
→ NUCLEAR STATE
→ CELLULAR STATE
→ DYNAMICAL TRAJECTORY
→ TRANSITION GEOMETRY
→ CEH HYPOTHESIS

Radiation-track research shows that biological response depends on spatial and temporal energy-deposition structure, not only absorbed dose. Monte Carlo track-structure and micro/nanodosimetry provide established ways to model these scales. ## Stochastic microscopic dynamics

Radiation-damage models have used stochastic processes, including continuous-time Markov models, to represent DNA double-strand-break formation, repair and chromosome-exchange processes. citeturn0search11

This suggests a richer synthetic world:

damage events → repair dynamics → residual nuclear state → cellular trajectory → transition probability

That is substantially more informative than adding generic Gaussian noise.

## Nuclear material physics

The cell nucleus is a dynamic physical material. Chromatin has viscoelastic properties, nuclear condensates can arise through phase separation, and mechanical and biochemical interactions influence nuclear organization. citeturn0search0turn0search1

This introduces additional candidate state variables:

- chromatin compaction;
- nuclear mechanical state;
- condensate number and size;
- spatial organization;
- phase-separation regime;
- DNA-damage burden;
- repair activity.

These are research variables, not assumptions that they constitute a CEH.

## Multiscale hypothesis

A future model could represent:

P_t = physical state
N_t = nuclear/chromatin state
B_t = cellular biological state
Y_t = observed measurements

with an observation relationship such as:

Y_t = h(P_t, N_t, B_t) + η_t

The purpose is to test whether information is lost when physical and nuclear variables are collapsed into a generic feature vector.

## Research questions

1. Does spatially structured physical perturbation alter transition geometry differently from homogeneous perturbation?
2. Can stochastic microscopic events generate reproducible macroscopic changes in state-space dynamics?
3. Does chromatin organization modify the mapping between physical perturbation and cellular state?
4. Can a transition signal survive independent observation modalities?
5. Does the physical layer improve prospective transition prediction beyond cellular state alone?
6. Can the same dynamical principle survive changes in perturbation scale?

## Evidence boundary

Radiation physics and nuclear biophysics provide established mechanisms and modelling tools. They do not validate CEH.

The project must distinguish:

physical mechanism
≠
nuclear response
≠
cellular transition
≠
CEH evidence

The strongest future experiment would specify the perturbation, microscopic mechanism, nuclear response, reconstructed cellular state, prospective endpoint and matched controls.

The goal is to determine whether the physical layer contains predictive or explanatory information that disappears when biology is represented only as an abstract state vector.
